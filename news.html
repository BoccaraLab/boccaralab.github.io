Welcome to Anastasia!

Welcome to Anastasia Tsopanidou, a visiting PhD student from the University of Copenhagen. She studies how local brain temperature is regulated by neuromodulatory and vascular dynamics during sleep and state transitions. Her work also explores how ambient temperature influences sleep drive, particularly in the context of neurodegenerative diseases. She is currently visiting our lab in Oslo for a 6-weeks period, and she will investigate how neuronal activity shapes local brain temperature across sleep and wake states.

Date: Friday, 20 June 2025 from 09:00 – 17:00

23 May 2025




Welcome to Alessandro!

Welcome to Alessandro Arena, our new researcher. He has a PhD in physiology, with competences in cognitive neuroscience, in-vivo electrophysiology, deep brain stimulation, sensory evoked potentials, data analysis and machine learning.
He previously worked for the international initiative “Human Brain Project” and covered the role of head of analytics for a consulting agency in web technology.
His scientific interest focused on conscious experience, exploring the related neuronal mechanisms and brain circuits. In particular, he studied how different brain states (e.g.: wakefulness, sleep, anaesthesia) modulate the propagation of information within the brain network across scales, from local neuronal units to global EEG. He will be supporting the Boccara lab organization with experiments, teaching and supervision. He will contribute to investigate how the communication across brain areas matures in postnatal development and the impact of sleep in such maturation.

    04 Oct, 2023




    New publication in IEEE Sensors Letters

    A new paper from Smartsense project titled Adipose tissue characterization with electrical impedance spectroscopy and machine learning is now out in IEEE Sensors Letters

    Electrical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) is a technique that allows to analyze a material by measuring its response to small electrical signals. In this work, we show that EIS can be used to discriminate different types of mouse fat tissue from one another. This is a first step towards complementing traditional tissue analysis methods in order to gain further insight on the structure and properties of various fat tissues.
